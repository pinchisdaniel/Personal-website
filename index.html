Games Created

Playable worlds I built, starting with my very first one.

Forged in Shadows

A horror adventure set in the Edo period, where you help a former blacksmith in his haunted workshop.

  • Unity
  • C#
  • Lighting
  • Team project

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Build my first playable horror game: a haunted blacksmith's workshop where the fear comes from atmosphere, pacing and detail instead of cheap jump scares.

It's only a prototype, but it's the project that got me hooked on making games.

How I Did It
  • Engine: built it in Unity, learning how to import assets and put together environments that actually run.
  • Scripting: wrote every interaction in C#, from doors that open to triggers that fire.
  • Lighting: tuned the shadows until the workshop felt creepy instead of just dark.
  • Teamwork: my friend Markus Mayrhofer handled the design and arrangements, while I did the scripting, lighting and some of the models, all synced through Unity Version Control.

Scene 02

Character Animations

Rigged and animated characters made in Blender, from simple cycles to full sequences.

Zombie Animations

A zombie character rigged and animated in Blender, with a set of movement cycles.

  • Blender
  • Rigging
  • Cycles

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Get comfortable with rigging and movement cycles by giving one character a full set of believable motions that blend smoothly into each other.

How I Did It
  • Rigging: created the rig with bones, controllers and IK/FK setups.
  • Cycles: animated idle, walk, run and turn cycles, then smoothed the transitions between them.
  • Principles: worked on timing and spacing so every motion has weight.

Stickman Test

A stickman movement test with jumps, vaults and rolls.

  • Blender
  • Body mechanics
  • Camera

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Push past basic cycles into fast, acrobatic movement that still feels believable.

How I Did It
  • Reference: got up from the chair and acted out the moves myself to understand how each joint moves.
  • Blocking: posed the key moments of every jump, vault and roll first.
  • Polish: refined timing, spacing and flow until the motion read clearly.
  • Camera: positioned and tracked the camera so the render feels natural.

Stickman Dance

A full dance sequence animated on a stickman rig.

  • Blender
  • Timing
  • Personality

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Animate a long, expressive sequence and give a simple character personality through motion alone.

How I Did It
  • Timing: applied timing and spacing across many different styles of motion.
  • Flow: kept the transitions between moves smooth so the dance never stops reading as one performance.
  • Camera: created smooth camera moves that follow the dancer.

Scene 03

3D Environments

Environment designs and models created in Blender.

Greek Temple Ruin

A classical temple surrounded by trees, lamps and cars, set against a night sky.

  • Blender
  • Modeling
  • Composition

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

My first environment project: learn composition, lighting and 3D by building a complete scene instead of a single object.

How I Did It
  • Modeling: built the classical architecture, from columns to stairs and arches.
  • Composition: arranged trees, bushes, street lamps, vehicles and a path so the scene feels alive.
  • Lighting: set it against a nighttime backdrop and experimented with spot, area and world lighting.

Snowday

A quiet winter scene with mountains, water and snowy pine trees. Yes, it's also the background of this site.

  • Blender
  • Nature
  • Lighting

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Work with the environment itself and create natural elements instead of buildings.

How I Did It
  • Landscape: shaped the mountains and the ground around them.
  • Nature: created the snow, the water and the snowed on pine trees.
  • Lighting: lit the scene with world lighting to sell the winter mood.

Hidden Ruins

A fantasy sword staged in hidden ruins with water, magic and god rays.

  • Blender
  • Lighting
  • Rendering

or click Danny on top of the card

The Goal

Set the perfect scene for a fantasy sword, so the whole environment guides your eye straight to it.

How I Did It
  • Props: dressed the ruins with environmental props.
  • Effects: added realistic water and a glowing magic circle.
  • Light: used god rays to frame the sword.
  • Rendering: tuned camera angle, depth of field and render settings to get quality without hours of render time.
